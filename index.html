LOFT / GOBELINS / MOUFFETARD

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Panthéon⌖
The great republican monument on the hill above the Loft: crypt, Foucault pendulum and an excellent way into the French idea of national memory. Close enough to be opportunistic rather than a major expedition.
60-90 min
Recommended
⌂Major attraction
Jardin des Plantes⌖
Large historic botanical garden with glasshouses, galleries and plenty of outdoor space. Useful when you want Paris to feel green for an hour without making a special cross-city journey.
45-120 min
No
◫Craft / activity
Manufacture des Gobelins⌖
A working tapestry manufacture with extraordinary traditional looms and craftspeople rather than a static museum. It is almost on the doorstep and particularly good if a guided workshop visit fits the day.
90 min
Yes
◫Neighbourhood
Butte-aux-Cailles⌖
Village-like streets, cafés and street art south-east of the Loft. A useful 45-minute potter when you want atmosphere rather than another formal attraction.
45-75 min
No
○Bakery
Le Fournil de Mouffetard⌖
Primary Sunday breakfast expedition: a well-regarded bakery on Rue Mouffetard, good enough to make the walk part of breakfast rather than simply solving the croissant problem.
10-20 min
No
○Bakery
La Gobelinoise⌖
The ultra-practical backup bakery on Avenue des Gobelins: very early opening and Sunday service make it the answer when somebody simply needs bread and pastries now.
10 min
No
○Market
Marché Monge⌖
Excellent Sunday-morning food market with fruit, cheese, prepared food and neighbourhood atmosphere. A very easy way to make breakfast/provisions feel like part of the holiday.
30-60 min
No
○Market
Rue Mouffetard⌖
Classic market street rather than a single attraction: food shops, stalls, bakeries and small businesses running north towards the Latin Quarter. Best treated as a wandering route.
30-60 min
No
○Treat
Mococha⌖
Small specialist chocolate and confectionery stop on Mouffetard. Exactly the sort of ten-minute edible side quest that can break up a walk without turning into another scheduled attraction.
10-15 min
No
＋Useful
La P'tite Cave⌖
Neighbourhood wine shop close to the Loft, with the bonus of useful Sunday-morning opening. Good for something interesting to take back to the apartment without making wine-shopping a project.
10-20 min
No
＋Useful
Franprix / Glacière⌖
Simple emergency groceries close to the Loft, useful on Sunday and late in the day when the objective is milk, snacks or forgotten essentials rather than a French-food experience.
10 min
No
⌁Eat - flexible
Le Vieux Bistrot⌖
A strong local safety-net: traditional French cooking on Mouffetard with broad, continuous service. This is the kind of place to use when everyone is hungry and you do not want dinner to become another planning exercise.
60-90 min
No
⌁Eat - proper
Les Baux de Paris⌖
Well-regarded neighbourhood French restaurant on Mouffetard. More of a deliberate meal than a fallback, but still relaxed enough to feel like a local dinner rather than an occasion restaurant.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - different
La Crète⌖
Highly rated Greek option on Mouffetard and a useful palate-change after a lot of French food. Broadly family-safe without feeling like a compromise restaurant.
60-75 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - ultra-flexible
Verse Toujours⌖
Long-hours neighbourhood brasserie at the bottom of Mouffetard. The important feature is operational: breakfast-to-late-night opening makes it a reliable 'food now' card.
45-75 min
No
⌁Eat - flexible
Le Mouffetard⌖
Historic neighbourhood brasserie with broad hours and continuous food service. Not necessarily a destination meal, but very useful when timing matters more than chasing the perfect restaurant.

LATIN QUARTER / NOTRE-DAME / ÎLES

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Notre-Dame de Paris⌖
The obvious eastern anchor for Day 1. Entry is free and the rebuilt cathedral is worth seeing inside; a free timed reservation can reduce queue uncertainty without turning it into a rigid appointment.
30-60 min
Recommended
⌂Major attraction
Île Saint-Louis⌖
Tiny island of handsome streets, food shops and river views immediately east of Notre-Dame. Worth walking through rather than merely looking at from a bridge.
30-45 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Sorbonne / Latin Quarter⌖
Not one ticketed sight so much as the historic university quarter itself: steep streets, bookshops and student Paris. Best experienced as the connective tissue between the Loft and the Seine.
30-60 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Panthéon⌖
Cross-reference from the Loft page: close enough to this axis that it can be added if enthusiasm and timing line up.
60-90 min
Recommended
◇Shop
Shakespeare and Company⌖
Eccentric English-language bookshop opposite Notre-Dame. Famous and sometimes queued, but still exactly the right kind of 20-minute retail side quest for this itinerary.
20-30 min
No
○Treat
Berthillon⌖
The classic Île Saint-Louis ice-cream name. Treat it as an opportunistic treat rather than a pilgrimage; if the original shop is shut or queued, plenty of nearby sellers carry the ice cream.
15-30 min
No
◫History / craft
Monnaie de Paris⌖
Historic mint on the Seine with coins, medals and metalworking. More tactile and specialised than another art museum, and the shop is good for unusual small objects and souvenirs.
60-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - proper
Le Petit Châtelet⌖
Tiny traditional French restaurant beside Shakespeare & Company. Geography is excellent without feeling like generic monument-frontage feeding; a strong deliberate lunch if the timing works.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - quick
La Crème de Paris Notre-Dame⌖
Crêpes, waffles and ice cream close to the cathedral. A deliberately easy Junior-friendly option when the immediate problem is morale and calories rather than achieving a memorable French lunch.
30-60 min
No
⌁Eat - flexible
Jardin Notre-Dame⌖
Straightforward French restaurant south of the cathedral with broad hours. Keep it for operational flexibility rather than because it is the area's great culinary destination.
60-75 min
No
⌁Eat - proper
La Rôtisserie d'Argent⌖
The serious nearby option: a genuine traditional restaurant beside one of Paris's biggest tourist magnets. Best used when you actually want a proper meal rather than a quick sightseeing lunch.
90 min
Yes
⌁Eat - island
Le Saint-Régis⌖
Attractive Île Saint-Louis brasserie with broad service hours. Useful when the island itself naturally becomes the lunch stop and you want something better than random tourist frontage.
60-75 min
No
⌁Eat - island
Les Fous de l'Île⌖
More polished island option with a broad menu and relaxed format. A good middle ground between a quick brasserie and a formal lunch.
75-90 min
Recommended
!Avoid
Notre-Dame frontage roulette⌖
Do not choose lunch solely because a restaurant is the first one visible from the cathedral or river. This area rewards walking five minutes sideways before committing.

SAINT-GERMAIN / LEFT BANK

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Jardin du Luxembourg⌖
One of the great Paris gardens and a genuinely useful outdoor pause: lawns, chairs, fountains and enough activity to work for adults and children without becoming a formal visit.
45-90 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Musée d'Orsay⌖
The obvious major museum on this side of the river. The building and concentrated Impressionist collection make it a better short-visit proposition than trying to conquer the Louvre.
90 min-3 hr
Recommended
◇Mad shop
Deyrolle⌖
Extraordinary historic natural-history and taxidermy shop: part shop, part cabinet of curiosities. One of the strongest non-attraction side quests in the whole directory; note that Sunday opening is the constraint.
20-30 min
No
◇Craft / shop
Sennelier⌖
Historic artists' materials shop on Quai Voltaire, selling pigments, paints, papers and specialist materials. Beautiful, specific and worth fifteen minutes even if nobody needs an oil-paint set.
15-25 min
No
◇Food shop
La Grande Épicerie de Paris⌖
The mad food hall: cheese, pâtisserie, wine, groceries, charcuterie and expensive edible objects under one roof. Treat it as an attraction for people who like food, not just a supermarket.
30-60 min
No
○Treat
Patrick Roger⌖
Chocolate as sculpture. The displays and bold flavours make this feel closer to a tiny gallery visit than a conventional chocolatier, while still ending with something edible.
10-20 min
No
○Treat
Le Chocolat Alain Ducasse⌖
Serious bean-to-bar chocolate in a compact Saint-Germain counter. Less theatrical than Patrick Roger, but excellent if Senior wants to compare the proper grown-up chocolate options.
10-20 min
No
○Treat
Pierre Hermé⌖
Macarons and polished modern pâtisserie. Useful as a quick morale stop because you can buy one thing and move on rather than committing to a café session.
10-20 min
No
○Bakery
Poilâne⌖
Historic wood-fired sourdough bakery on Rue du Cherche-Midi, still run as a serious craft bakery rather than a nostalgia set. Best if passing on Saturday; Sunday is the practical limitation.
10-15 min
No
○Wine
La Dernière Goutte⌖
Excellent independent wine shop in Saint-Germain. Friendly enough for a quick bottle recommendation and useful because it does not require turning wine into a specialist expedition.
15-20 min
No
⌁Eat - modern
Semilla⌖
Serious contemporary French cooking on Rue de Seine without becoming ceremonial fine dining. A strong deliberate lunch or dinner when you want the food itself to matter.
90-120 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - traditional
La Jacobine⌖
Cosy classic French in the Cour du Commerce Saint-André: onion soup, confit and comfort-food territory. Particularly useful because lunch can stretch later than at many Paris restaurants.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - flexible
La Boussole⌖
Well-regarded French restaurant near Saint-Sulpice with later lunch slots often available. A good compromise between proper food and a sightseeing day that refuses to run exactly on time.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - Junior-safe
Breizh Café⌖
Proper Breton galettes and crêpes rather than a children's-menu compromise. Very easy family lunch and a good reset if everyone wants something straightforward but still distinctly French.
60-75 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - experience
Le Relais de l'Entrecôte⌖
The famous single-concept steak, sauce, fries and seconds. Fun if everybody actively wants that exact experience; the downside is the queue and complete lack of menu flexibility.
75-90 min
No
⌁Eat - historic
Brasserie Lipp⌖
Historic brasserie saturated with French political and literary history. Senior may enjoy the roll-call of politicians and writers almost as much as the food; use it knowingly for atmosphere.
75-90 min
Recommended
○Coffee / theatre
Café de Flore⌖
Iconic Saint-Germain terrace. Worth having coffee or a drink for the cultural theatre; no need to ask it to provide the best-value lunch of the holiday.
20-40 min
No
○Coffee / theatre
Les Deux Magots⌖
Same principle as Flore: famous, historic and worth seeing, but best treated as a short atmospheric stop rather than an obligatory expensive meal.
20-40 min
No

LOUVRE / PALAIS-ROYAL / OPÉRA / MADELEINE

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Louvre⌖
The major museum, but not a compulsory full-day project. The courtyard, pyramid and palace are valuable even if you decide the family does not need several hours inside.
30 min-4 hr
Yes if entering
⌂Major attraction
Palais-Royal⌖
Elegant courtyard, gardens and arcades beside the Louvre. Excellent decompression from the crowds and a natural base for several of the craft/shop side quests below.
20-40 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Place Vendôme⌖
Grand formal square of jewellery houses and state history. Best as a ten-minute orientation stop while moving between Tuileries, Opéra and Madeleine.
10-20 min
No
◇Craft / shop
Maison Fabre⌖
Historic French glove maker in the Palais-Royal arcades. Exactly the kind of living craft retail that is enjoyable to browse even if nobody arrives intending to buy gloves.
10-20 min
No
◇Craft / shop
Charvet⌖
Legendary Paris shirtmaker on Place Vendôme. More of a look at serious tailoring and fabric culture than a shopping requirement, but very much in the artisanal lane.
10-20 min
No
◇Leather
Moynat⌖
Historic French trunk and leather house with beautiful construction and materials. Less obvious than some luxury names and useful as a quick look at high-end leather craft.
10-20 min
No
◇Mad shop
E. Dehillerin⌖
Old-school professional cookware shop near Les Halles: copper pans, knives and restaurant equipment stacked with very little concession to luxury-retail theatre. Excellent weird-shop material.
20-30 min
No
＋Passage
Galerie Vivienne⌖
Probably the prettiest covered passage: mosaic floors, books, shops and architecture. A perfect 20-minute detour that feels distinctively Parisian without requiring a ticket.
20-30 min
No
＋Passage
Passage des Panoramas⌖
Older, denser and more eccentric than Vivienne, with restaurants, stamps and small shops. It naturally links the central-city walk towards Grands Boulevards and Montmartre.
20-30 min
No
＋Passage
Passage Jouffroy⌖
Continuation north from Panoramas with toys, bookish shops and the Musée Grévin frontage. Best used as part of a passage chain rather than a standalone destination.
15-25 min
No
◇Food shop
Fauchon Madeleine⌖
The Hannibal Lecter literary side quest and a historic luxury épicerie name. Go for the association, packaging and edible oddities rather than expecting the largest food hall in Paris.
15-30 min
No
○Treat
À la Mère de Famille⌖
Historic confectioner dating from the eighteenth century, with a wonderfully old-fashioned shop and lots of sweets and chocolate. One of the best family side quests on the Opéra-to-Montmartre axis.
15-25 min
No
○Treat
Angelina⌖
Famous Rivoli tearoom for hot chocolate and Mont-Blanc. Worth it if the queue looks sane; not worth sacrificing an hour of Paris merely to prove you have been.
30-45 min
No
◫Activity - Junior
La Tête dans les Nuages⌖
Huge arcade/games venue and an excellent emergency card if Junior has reached the end of her cultural tolerance or the weather turns ugly. Not remotely subtle, which is part of the point.
60-120 min
No
⌁Eat - flexible
Le Relais du Louvre⌖
Useful near-Louvre restaurant with broad hours and sensible positioning. A strong 'we are hungry here' option that avoids gambling on the first café facing the monument.
60-75 min
No
⌁Eat - proper
Le Louis⌖
Small French restaurant north of Les Halles with a relatively forgiving Sunday lunch window. Good when you want a real meal without converting lunch into a two-hour event.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - atmosphere
Le Grand Colbert⌖
Glorious Belle-Époque brasserie interior near Galerie Vivienne. Choose it when Parisian brasserie atmosphere is part of what you are buying, not because you are chasing culinary novelty.
90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - flexible
Bistrot Vivienne⌖
Convenient brasserie woven directly into the covered-passages geography. Particularly useful when wandering rather than reservations is driving the day.
60-75 min
No
○Coffee
Le Nemours⌖
Small Palais-Royal corner café that is ideal for coffee and people-watching. A good example of paying a little for famous geography without asking it to carry a whole meal.
20-30 min
No
!Avoid
Café Marly as default lunch⌖
The Louvre view is spectacular, but the setting is what you are paying for. Use it only if sitting under the arcades looking at the pyramid is itself the objective.

CHAMPS / ÉLYSÉE / INVALIDES / EIFFEL

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Champs-Élysées⌖
The famous avenue is principally an axis for this trip: walk some of it, use Sephora, see the Arc, then move sideways for food rather than treating every frontage as a recommendation.
30-60 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Arc de Triomphe⌖
Strong western anchor for Day 1. The rooftop is genuinely worthwhile if weather and legs cooperate, but the monument still works as an exterior/underpass stop if the day is already long.
45-75 min
Recommended
⌂Major attraction
Eiffel Tower⌖
An icon rather than an obligation to ascend. Trocadéro and Champ de Mars views may be enough; if you do want the tower itself, pre-book because spontaneous ticket queues can consume the evening.
30 min-2 hr
Yes if ascending
⌂Major attraction
Trocadéro⌖
The classic broad view of the Eiffel Tower and a useful place to decide whether the family still wants more sightseeing or simply wants food/boat/home.
20-40 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Invalides⌖
Major military/state complex founded by Louis XIV. The value for this family is the history of French power well beyond Napoleon: monarchy, Revolution, republic, world wars and de Gaulle.
60 min-3 hr
Recommended
⌂Major attraction
Place de la Concorde⌖
Central piece of the east-west axis and a compact way into Revolution, monarchy and modern state symbolism. Better as a ten-minute historical pause than a formal visit.
10-20 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Palais de l'Élysée⌖
The presidential palace is mainly exterior/political geography on an ordinary weekend. For Senior, the surrounding Faubourg Saint-Honoré and Place Beauvau make the French state feel physically real.
20-30 min
No
◫Politics / shop
Maison Élysée⌖
Presidency-themed exhibition, café and shop opposite the Élysée. A wonderfully specific Senior side quest combining politics, French-made products and official-state merchandising.
20-40 min
No
◇Shop - Junior
Sephora Champs-Élysées⌖
The correct Sephora for Junior: the flagship on the avenue, huge enough to make the shop itself part of the experience and directly on the Day 1 route.
30-60 min
No
◇Craft / shop
Boutique Talents / Ateliers d'Art de France⌖
Curated French artisan pieces - jewellery, ceramics, leather and small-batch objects - near the Arc/Ternes area. A more attainable way to see contemporary craft than luxury-house browsing.
20-30 min
No
◫Boat
Bateaux-Mouches⌖
Best used as tired-leg theatre rather than transport: one hour on the Seine, ideally late afternoon or dusk, with everyone outside looking at Paris instead of concentrating on the commentary.
1 hr
Buy ahead
◫Boat
Bateaux Parisiens⌖
Eiffel-foot alternative to Bateaux-Mouches. Useful if you reach the western end of Day 1 and want the boat immediately without adding another transfer.
1 hr
Recommended
⌁Eat - flexible
Chez Pippo⌖
Italian near the Eiffel Tower with broad continuous hours. Pizza and pasta make it a very strong tactical family option when everyone is done with sightseeing and wants food without ceremony.
60-75 min
No
⌁Eat - experience
Riviera Fuga⌖
Restaurant on the Seine at Port des Invalides. Stylish and unusual enough that the setting contributes to the experience, while still being more playful than another grand brasserie.
90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - setting
Francette⌖
Floating restaurant at Port de Suffren beside the Eiffel Tower. Particularly useful as a flexible endpoint to a long walking day because the river setting feels like part of the sightseeing.
75-90 min
Recommended
!Avoid
Random Champs/Eiffel frontage⌖
The avenue and tower approaches are excellent for sights, but poor places to choose a restaurant solely by what is directly in front of you. Walk a few minutes sideways first.

MONTMARTRE / PIGALLE

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Sacré-Cœur⌖
The obvious hilltop anchor, with the panorama doing as much work as the basilica itself. Do not let Sacré-Cœur become the whole of Montmartre; the streets around it are the real point.
30-45 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Montmartre⌖
The neighbourhood itself is the attraction: steep streets, stairs, shops, cafés and changing views. Build time for wandering rather than hopping directly between named sights.
60-120 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Place du Tertre⌖
Artist-filled square near Sacré-Cœur. Enjoy the theatre and people-watching, but treat the restaurant frontage with caution and consult the food options below before sitting down.
15-30 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Moulin Rouge⌖
Iconic Pigalle exterior and useful orientation landmark. Unless you specifically want the show, this is a quick look rather than an evening commitment.
10-15 min
No
◫Museum / garden
Musée de Montmartre⌖
Small neighbourhood museum and Renoir gardens that explain the artistic village without requiring the scale or energy of a major museum. Particularly good if Montmartre itself has caught everyone's interest.
60-90 min
Recommended
◫Walk
Abbesses - Lepic - upper Montmartre⌖
A route rather than a sight: start around Abbesses and Rue Lepic, then work uphill through shops and streets. This is the best antidote to treating Montmartre as one photo at Sacré-Cœur.
60-90 min
No
◇Mad shop / art
Halle Saint-Pierre⌖
Outsider and popular-art centre below Sacré-Cœur with an unusually good specialist bookshop. Excellent if Senior wants something odd and Junior is bored by conventional galleries.
30-60 min
No
○Treat
Sébastien Gaudard - Rue des Martyrs⌖
Elegant classic pâtisserie on the natural route between Grands Boulevards/Pigalle and Montmartre. A high-quality morale stop rather than an attraction that needs scheduling.
10-20 min
No
○Treat
À la Mère de Famille⌖
Cross-reference from the Opéra page: historic confectioner on the natural approach to Montmartre, and probably one of the easiest family side quests to say yes to.
15-25 min
No
○Coffee / light food
Rose Bakery - Rue des Martyrs⌖
Easy café/tea-room stop on the Martyrs axis. Best when you want something lighter than a full French restaurant and the day's timing is deliberately loose.
45-60 min
No
⌁Eat - proper/flexible
Le Poulbot⌖
One of the strongest Montmartre finds: proper French food right in the neighbourhood with broad continuous service. A very good default when you want quality without timing anxiety.
75-90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - proper
L'Annexe Montmartre⌖
Small highly rated French restaurant on Rue des Trois Frères. Better for a deliberate meal than a late-lunch fallback, so use it when the day's timing is behaving.
90 min
Recommended
⌁Eat - flexible
Gavroche⌖
Near Sacré-Cœur with uninterrupted service, making it useful insurance on a day when Montmartre wandering can easily push lunch later than intended.
60-90 min
No / Recommended
⌁Eat - cheap/fun
Bouillon Pigalle⌖
Big, noisy, inexpensive bouillon with traditional dishes and lots of energy. Junior-compatible and deliberately unprecious; an excellent reset if everyone wants to sit down now.
60-75 min
No
⌁Eat - flexible
La Boîte aux Lettres⌖
Rue Lepic restaurant with broad hours and good geography for the actual wandering route. Useful when you want something more intimate than Bouillon Pigalle without needing a formal booking.
60-75 min
No
!Avoid
Place du Tertre restaurant roulette⌖
The square is worth seeing; choosing the first restaurant because it occupies the square is a separate decision. Enjoy the artists, then use the directory before sitting down.

MARAIS / BASTILLE

⌖ MAP
⌂Major attraction
Place des Vosges⌖
Beautiful seventeenth-century square and a very easy reason to wander into the Marais. The arcades and small garden work well as a pause rather than a formal visit.
20-40 min
No
⌂Major attraction
Musée Carnavalet⌖
Excellent museum of Paris itself, housed in historic mansions. A strong rainy-day/history option because the subject is the city you are actually walking through.
60-120 min
Recommended
○Market / food
Marché des Enfants Rouges⌖
Paris's oldest covered market: produce plus small food counters and international lunch options. Works as both a market side quest and a flexible meal rather than a restaurant booking.
45-90 min
No
○Treat
Edwart Chocolatier - Marais⌖
Small artisan chocolatier with imaginative flavours and a less corporate feel than the grand luxury houses. Good for a quick comparison stop in a district already full of retail temptation.
10-20 min
No
◇Shop
Merci⌖
Large concept store mixing design, homewares, fashion and café culture. A modern counterpart to the historic craft shops elsewhere in the directory.
30-45 min
No
◇Shop - Junior
Bonton⌖
Children's concept store with clothes, objects and playful retail presentation. More interesting than generic kids' shopping and a plausible Junior side quest if you are already in the Marais.
20-30 min
No
◫Mad museum
Musée de la Chasse et de la Nature⌖
Art, animals and natural history presented with deliberate eccentricity. Much more likely to hold family attention than a conventional gallery and very much in the Deyrolle-adjacent universe.
60-90 min
Recommended
○Eat - market
Marché des Enfants Rouges counters⌖
Best if everyone wants different things or timing is awkward: choose from market counters rather than committing to a single restaurant and keep the whole stop casual.
45-75 min
No
⌁Eat - Junior-safe
Breizh Café Marais⌖
Cross-reference to the Saint-Germain favourite: galettes and crêpes are an easy family choice, and the Marais branch can rescue the geography without changing the concept.
60-75 min
Recommended
